What counts as adulteration

Not spoilage. Not an accident. A substitution.

Food adulteration is when a substance is added, removed, or substituted to make a product look bigger, brighter, heavier, or cheaper to produce — without the buyer's knowledge. It ranges from watering down milk to mixing industrial dye into turmeric.

It thrives on invisibility: adulterated food usually looks, and often tastes, completely normal. That's exactly why a five-minute kitchen test matters more than a glance at the package.

Why community programmes matter: a single household test protects one family. A neighbourhood that knows the tests — and knows the local vendor, dairy, and grocer — makes adulteration far less profitable to attempt in the first place.

Run the test yourself

Tap a test to open it, then reveal what a positive result looks like
01Water in milk — the slant test
+
Method

Let a drop of milk fall onto a polished, slightly tilted surface, like a steel plate.

DILUTED

Runs quickly, leaves no trail. Pure milk flows slowly and leaves a thin white streak.

02Starch in milk — the iodine test
+
Method

Add two drops of iodine tincture to a small sample of milk in a clear glass.

STARCH FOUND

Colour turns blue. Pure milk keeps the iodine's original amber-brown colour.

03Detergent in milk — the shake test
+
Method

Mix equal parts milk and water in a bottle, close it, and shake well for a few seconds.

DETERGENT FOUND

Thick, soapy, long-lasting foam forms. Pure milk produces only a thin, quickly-settling layer.

04Dye in turmeric powder
+
Method

Sprinkle a pinch of turmeric powder onto a glass of plain water and let it sit, undisturbed.

DYED

Water instantly turns bright orange-red. Pure turmeric colours the water slowly and mildly.

05Artificial colour in chilli powder
+
Method

Sprinkle a pinch of chilli powder onto the surface of a glass of water.

DYED

A streak of colour bleeds off immediately. Pure powder floats and colours the water only gradually.

06Chalk powder in sugar
+
Method

Dissolve a spoon of sugar completely in a glass of water and let it stand for a minute.

CHALK FOUND

A white, gritty layer settles at the bottom. Pure sugar dissolves fully, leaving clear water.

07Sugar syrup in honey
+
Method

Drop a spoon of honey gently into a glass of water without stirring.

DILUTED

It dissolves quickly, clouding the water. Pure honey stays mostly intact and settles as a lump.

08Chicory in coffee powder
+
Method

Sprinkle a pinch of coffee powder onto the surface of a glass of water and watch closely.

CHICORY FOUND

Chicory sinks fast, leaving a brown trail. Pure coffee floats on the surface for longer.

09Dye on dal / lentils
+
Method

Rub a small handful of dal between your wet palms for a few seconds.

DYED

Colour transfers onto your palms. Undyed dal leaves your hands clean.

10Impurities in food grains
+
Method

Spread a small quantity of grains thinly on a clean, transparent glass plate and look closely.

CONTAMINATED

Visible stones, dust, husk, or mismatched grains stand out. Pure grain looks uniform in size and colour.

Why this isn't just a quality issue

Adulterants are chosen to be cheap and invisible — not to be safe

Digestive & organ damage

Chalk, brick powder, and industrial dyes are not designed to be eaten. Regular exposure strains the gut, liver, and kidneys.

Long-term toxicity

Lead chromate and metanil yellow accumulate in the body over time and are linked to serious long-term health effects.

Epidemic dropsy

Argemone oil in mustard oil has triggered documented outbreaks of this severe, sometimes fatal condition in India.

Higher risk for children

Children, pregnant women, and the elderly are more vulnerable to the same dose that a healthy adult might tolerate.

Found something adulterated?

Don't just stop buying it — report it

Call the FSSAI helpline

Toll-free, for any food safety complaint anywhere in India.

1800-11-2100

File it properly

  1. Stop using the product immediately.
  2. Keep the bill, packaging, and a sample if possible.
  3. Note the brand, batch number, and manufacturing date.
  4. File a complaint via FSSAI's Food Safety Connect portal or your local Food Safety Officer.

Bringing this to your community

This guide is built to run as a live session — a housing-society meeting, a market-day stall, or a school programme. Bring the ingredients, run the tests in front of people, and let the results speak.

Jump to the test list
  • Set up a "live lab" table: milk, iodine, turmeric, chilli powder, sugar, honey, water, clear glasses.
  • Run 3–4 tests live and let attendees try the next one themselves.
  • Hand out a printed version of the ten tests as a take-home reference.
  • Share the FSSAI helpline number on a poster near the exit.
  • Close with a short pledge: check one staple food a week for a month.
